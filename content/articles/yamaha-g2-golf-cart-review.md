The Yamaha G2 is a classic Yamaha golf car built from **1985 to 1991**, in **gas** and **36-volt electric** versions. Today it's a budget runabout or a project cart. Solid ones are still worth buying **if the price reflects their age** and you check the frame, steering and brakes carefully.

## Identify a G2 by its serial number

The serial plate is usually **under the seat towards the front** or **on the frame under the front bumper**.

| Version | Serial prefix | Years |
|---|---|---|
| Gas | **J38**, then **JA2** from 1988 | 1985–1991 |
| Electric | **J41**, then **JE2** from 1988 | 1985–1991 |

If the prefix doesn't match, it isn't a G2, whatever the seller says. Later G-series models (G8, G9, G14, G16 and so on) have their own prefixes.

## Size and weight

| Spec | Yamaha G2 (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Length | ~90 in |
| Width | ~46.5 in |
| Height (roof) | ~67.5 in |
| Weight (electric, 36 V) | ~880 lb |

It's slightly smaller than modern carts. See how it compares in our [golf cart dimensions guide](/shop/golf-cart-dimensions).

## What tends to go wrong

- **Electric:** a tired 36 V battery pack (budget for a new set unless there are receipts), corroded cables and solenoid problems
- **Gas:** carburettor varnish after storage, worn clutches and belts, and starter-generator brushes
- **Both:** steering play, weak brakes, worn bushings and rust on the frame around the battery tray or engine mount

## What to pay

There's no blue book for a cart this old. Price it on condition:

- **Running, tidy, recent batteries/service:** a fair price for a basic used cart
- **Runs but needs batteries or brakes:** deduct the cost of those parts
- **Non-runner or rusty frame:** parts value or a project price only

Bring a multimeter for electric carts. Our [battery test guide](/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-golf-cart-battery-is-bad) takes ten minutes and can save you hundreds.

## Should you upgrade it?

Popular G2 projects include LED lights, new seats and a lift kit. On gas carts, a [governor adjustment](/shop/yamaha-golf-cart-governor-adjustment) adds a little speed, but old brakes and steering are the limit. Fix those first. If you'd spend more on upgrades than a newer used cart costs, buy the newer cart.
