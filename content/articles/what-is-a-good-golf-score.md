For an adult recreational golfer, **breaking 100 is a solid score, breaking 90 is good and breaking 80 is excellent.** The average US golfer shoots **about 94** for 18 holes, according to the USGA. Only **about 26%** of adult golfers average under 90, and **about 5%** average under 80.

So yes, **85 is a good score**. If that's your normal round, you're ahead of roughly three out of four golfers.

## Where your score sits

| Typical 18-hole score | Share of adult golfers who average better | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Under 70 | under 2% | Elite amateur / plus handicap |
| Under 80 | ~5% | Excellent, single-digit territory |
| Under 90 | ~26% | Good, a solid club golfer |
| Under 100 | ~55% | Respectable, the first big milestone |
| 100+ | ~45% | Most beginners and occasional players |

Percentages are based on National Golf Foundation data for scores people *typically* shoot, not their best-ever round.

## Score vs handicap

Scores depend on the course. 85 on a long, tight championship course is a better round than 85 on a short resort course. That's why golf uses the **Handicap Index**, which adjusts each score for course difficulty using the course rating and slope.

The USGA puts the average Handicap Index at **14.2 for men** and **28.7 for women**. Plug a few of your scores into our [handicap calculator](/shop/golf-handicap-calculator) to see where you land.

Rough guide on an average-difficulty course (rating ~71, slope ~125), assuming your best rounds are 2–3.5 strokes better than your average:

| Average score | Approx. Handicap Index |
|---|---|
| 75 | ~0–2 |
| 80 | ~5–6 |
| 85 | ~9–11 |
| 90 | ~14–15 |
| 95 | ~18–20 |
| 100 | ~23–24 |

Because your index uses your *best* recent rounds, it usually comes out a few strokes lower than your average score suggests.

## Good targets by experience

- **First season:** finish holes without a time limit and post scores. 110–120 is normal at first.
- **Year one to two:** break 100. Most of the gain comes from fewer penalty shots and three-putts.
- **Regular golfer:** break 90. Typically means hitting more greens from 150 yards in and getting up and down occasionally.
- **Keen club golfer:** break 80. Needs consistent ball-striking and a reliable short game.

## The fastest ways to lower your score

1. **Stop the big numbers.** Take your medicine after a bad shot and chip back to the fairway. One triple bogey ruins a card more than three bogeys.
2. **Count your putts.** Most mid-handicappers lose more strokes to three-putts than to drives. Practise speed from 30–40 ft.
3. **Play the right tees.** Too far back turns par 4s into par 5s.
4. **Track simple stats:** fairways, greens, putts and penalties. Our [guide to lowering your handicap](/blog/how-to-lower-your-golf-handicap) shows what to do with them.
