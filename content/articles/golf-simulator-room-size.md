For a comfortable home golf simulator, plan on a **ceiling of 9–10 ft or more**, a **width of 10–12 ft** (14 ft+ if left- and right-handers will share it) and a **depth of 15–20 ft**. Depth depends on your launch monitor: radar units that sit behind you need several extra feet.

Ceiling height is the dealbreaker in most rooms, and there's only one reliable test: **take your driver into the room and make some slow, full practice swings.** The calculator below gives you a starting point before you do.

## The three measurements explained

**Ceiling height.** Most simulator guides put the minimum at **8–9 ft** with limits (shorter players, flatter swings, no driver), and the recommendation at **10 ft or more** for full driver swings and taller golfers. Our calculator uses *your height plus your driver's length* as a conservative rule of thumb. Many swings need less because the club doesn't pass straight overhead, so treat a small shortfall as a reason to swing-test, not to give up.

**Width.** With only right-handed (or only left-handed) players, you can offset the hitting mat to one side and work with **10–12 ft**. For mixed groups the mat needs to be centred, which takes **14 ft or more** to swing safely on both sides.

**Depth.** A typical layout:

| Zone | Minimum | Comfortable |
|---|---|---|
| Behind the screen (so it can flex) | ~1 ft | ~1 ft |
| Ball to screen | ~10 ft | 10–12 ft |
| Behind the ball (your swing + launch monitor) | ~4 ft | ~7 ft |
| **Total** | **~15 ft** | **18–20 ft** |

## Launch monitor placement changes the room

| Launch monitor type | Where it sits | Space impact |
|---|---|---|
| Photometric, beside the ball (e.g. SkyTrak, Bushnell) | Next to the hitting area | Standard depth works |
| Ceiling-mounted camera (e.g. Uneekor) | Overhead, ~9–10 ft up, slightly in front of the tee | Needs enough ceiling height to mount above the swing path |
| Radar behind the golfer (e.g. Garmin R10, Mevo+) | Roughly 6–10 ft behind the tee, depending on model | Adds several feet of depth |

If your room is short on depth, a side-mounted photometric unit is usually the best fit. Our [launch monitor guide](/shop/best-launch-monitors-for-golf) compares the options.

## Screen and projector

- **Screen size:** often 10–12 ft wide and 7–9 ft tall in home setups, sized to your room.
- **Projector distance:** depends on the model's throw ratio. Short-throw projectors mounted overhead avoid your shadow on the screen. See our [simulator projector guide](/shop/best-golf-sim-projector).
- **Enclosure:** a frame with side and top netting protects walls and lights from mishits. Our [enclosure guide](/shop/best-golf-enclosure) covers sizes.

## Room-by-room reality check

- **Garage:** often the best option, but check the door track and opener height, and think about heat and cold for the electronics.
- **Basement:** usually long enough, but low ceilings and beams are the common problem. Swing-test under the lowest beam.
- **Spare bedroom:** often too short and too low for full driver swings. Fine for irons, wedges and putting with a smaller screen.
