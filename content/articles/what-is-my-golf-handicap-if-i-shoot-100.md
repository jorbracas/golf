If 100 is your normal score, your Handicap Index will usually land **somewhere around 22–26**. The exact number depends on the **course rating and slope** of the courses you play and on how much your scores vary. The handicap system averages your *best* rounds, so the occasional 94 pulls your index down more than a bad 108 pushes it up.

## Worked examples: a score of 100 on different courses

Differential = (100 − course rating) × 113 ÷ slope

| Course | Rating / slope | Differential for a 100 |
|---|---|---|
| Short, easy municipal | 68.0 / 113 | 32.0 |
| Average parkland course | 71.0 / 125 | 26.2 |
| Tougher course | 72.5 / 135 | 23.0 |
| Championship tees | 74.5 / 145 | 19.9 |

The same 100 is worth very different amounts. Shooting 100 from the championship tees of a hard course is a better round than 92 on an easy one.

## From differentials to your index

Say your last 20 rounds were all on an average course (71.0 / 125), mostly around 100, with a few good days:

- Best 8 scores: 93, 94, 95, 95, 96, 96, 97, 97
- Their differentials: 19.9, 20.8, 21.7, 21.7, 22.6, 22.6, 23.5, 23.5
- Average: **22.0**, so your **Handicap Index is 22.0**

Same golfer, same average score, but the index reflects the good days. That's by design: it measures your potential, not your average.

## Getting from 100 to the 90s

At this level the quickest gains rarely come from swing changes:

1. **Cut penalty shots.** Use a club you can keep in play off the tee, even if it's a hybrid.
2. **Avoid the second chip.** Get every chip onto the green, even if it finishes 20 ft away.
3. **Lag putts.** Aim to leave every long putt inside a 3-ft circle. Three-putts are the hidden killer at the 100 level.
4. **Play the right tees** for your distance.
