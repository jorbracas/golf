A good chip is a small, simple motion. **Narrow stance, weight about 55–65% on your lead side, ball around the middle of the stance, hands slightly ahead of the ball, and a rocking stroke with the shoulders, not the wrists.** Keep your weight forward from start to finish and let the loft of the club do the lifting.

## Chipping vs pitching

A **chip** is a short shot from just off the green that spends more time rolling than in the air. A **pitch** is longer and higher, with more wrist hinge. This guide covers the chip.

## The setup

1. **Stance**: narrower than a full swing, feet a few inches apart. Some coaches like the feet and hips slightly open to the target.
2. **Weight**: about **55–65% on your lead foot**, and it stays there.
3. **Ball position**: around the middle, or slightly back of middle. Too far back makes the leading edge dig. Our [ball position chart](/blog/golf-ball-position-chart) covers every club.
4. **Hands**: slightly ahead of the ball so the shaft leans a little toward the target. Not too far, or the leading edge digs in.
5. **Grip**: choke down the club for control.

## The stroke

- **Rock the shoulders.** Think of the arms and shoulders as a triangle that moves together, like a putting stroke with a bit more length.
- **Keep the wrists quiet.** A small amount of hinge happens naturally; don't add any.
- **Low point opposite your lead side.** Keep your chest moving toward the target so the club bottoms out at or just after the ball.
- **Don't freeze your head.** Many coaches suggest letting your eyes follow the ball after impact; staring hard at the ground can encourage a flick.
- **Same tempo back and through.** Control distance with the length of the backswing, not with how hard you hit.

## Which club should you chip with?

The simplest rule: **use the least loft that gets the ball safely onto the green**, then let it roll.

| Situation | Club |
|---|---|
| Lots of green to work with, nothing to carry | 8-iron, 9-iron or pitching wedge |
| Moderate green, small carry | Pitching or gap wedge |
| Short side, need to stop it quickly | Sand or lob wedge |
| Ball on the fringe with a tight lie | Consider the putter |

Low-lofted chip-and-runs are more forgiving because a slight mishit still rolls close. Our [wedge degrees guide](/shop/golf-wedge-degrees) explains how loft changes the carry-to-roll ratio.

## Common chipping mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Weight moves back | Fat or thin chips | Keep 55–65% on the lead foot throughout |
| Wrist flick at impact | Inconsistent contact | Rock the shoulders, keep the wrists quiet |
| Ball too far back, hands too far forward | Leading edge digs in | Ball near the middle, slight shaft lean only |
| Decelerating | Chunks and duffs | Shorter backswing, accelerate through |
| Always using a lob wedge | Hard to judge distance | Use less loft when you can |

## Four chipping drills

1. **Lead-hand-on-thigh drill**: place your lead hand on your thigh and chip with the trail hand only. It teaches the club to fall onto the ball without flicking.
2. **Towel target**: lay a towel on the green where you want the ball to land and count how many you land on it out of ten.
3. **One-club, three-distances**: chip with the same club to three different flags, changing only the backswing length.
4. **Bad lies**: practise from fluffy grass, tight lies and slopes, not just perfect ones.
