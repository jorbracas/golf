Match the flex to your **driver swing speed**. **Under 72 mph: ladies (L). 72–83 mph: senior (A). 84–96 mph: regular (R). 97–104 mph: stiff (S). 105 mph and up: extra stiff (X).** Speed is the starting point, then tempo and feel fine-tune it.

## Shaft flex chart

| Flex | Driver swing speed | Approx. driver carry |
|---|---|---|
| Ladies (L) | under 72 mph | under ~165 yd |
| Senior / A-flex | 72–83 mph | ~165–190 yd |
| Regular (R) | 84–96 mph | ~190–220 yd |
| Stiff (S) | 97–104 mph | ~220–240 yd |
| Extra stiff (X) | 105+ mph | ~240+ yd |

The speed bands are the driver figures GOLF.com published from True Spec Golf fitting data. The carry column is our rough conversion at about 2.3 yards per mph, a typical figure for amateurs. It's a guide if you've never measured your speed, but distance depends on strike as well as speed.

## How to find your swing speed

- **A launch monitor** at a range, shop or fitting gives the most reliable number.
- **A [swing speed radar](/shop/best-golf-swing-speed-tracker)** is a cheap way to measure it at home or on the range.
- **No gadget?** Use your average driver carry and the chart above, or our [club distance calculator](/shop/golf-club-distance-chart) in reverse.

Measure a dozen swings and use the average, not the best.

## Signs your flex is wrong

| What you see | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Low flight, shots can leak right (right-hander), feels "boardy" | Possibly too stiff |
| Ballooning flight, can hook, inconsistent distance | Possibly too soft |
| Good contact but big distance spread | Possibly wrong weight or flex, so get fitted |

## Speed isn't everything

Two golfers with the same speed can suit different shafts:

- **Tempo and transition.** A quick, aggressive change of direction loads the shaft harder, so it often suits one flex stiffer. A smooth tempo often suits one flex softer.
- **Weight.** A lighter shaft (50–60 g in a driver) helps slower swingers. Heavier shafts (65 g+) help stronger players control the club.
- **Kick point and torque.** These affect launch and feel; see [stiff vs regular shafts](/shop/stiff-vs-regular-golf-shaft) for a deeper comparison.

## Irons vs driver

Iron shafts follow the same logic, but use your **6- or 7-iron speed** if you can measure it. Most golfers use the same flex through the set, but many seniors play a regular-flex driver with steel or graphite irons in a softer flex. Our [iron shaft guide](/shop/best-golf-iron-shafts) and [driver shaft guide](/shop/best-golf-shaft-for-driver) go through the options.

## One warning on labels

There's **no industry standard** for flex. One brand's regular can be stiffer than another's stiff. Use the chart to narrow it down, then hit the actual shaft before buying.
