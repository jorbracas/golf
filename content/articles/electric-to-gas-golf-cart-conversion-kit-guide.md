Short answer: you can convert an electric golf cart to gas, but very few people should. It isn't a kit you bolt on in an afternoon. You're replacing the whole drivetrain, and the bill usually ends up close to the price of a decent used gas cart.

Most products sold as "golf cart conversion kits" are actually **engine swap kits for carts that are already gas**: a mounting plate, a clutch and hardware to drop a bigger engine (often a Predator 420 or 670) into an EZGO, Club Car or Yamaha gas frame. If your cart is electric, check the listing carefully before you buy.

## Why an electric cart can't just take an engine

An electric cart and a gas cart of the same model look alike, but underneath they work differently:

- **The rear end is different.** On an electric cart the motor bolts straight onto the differential. A gas cart uses a transaxle with an input shaft for the driven clutch.
- **Reverse works differently.** Electric carts reverse by spinning the motor the other way. A gas engine only turns one way, so you need a gearbox with forward, neutral and reverse.
- **The frame is often different.** Engine mounts, fuel tank space and the rear subframe usually aren't shared between the electric and gas versions of a model.
- **You need a whole 12-volt system.** That means a starter or starter-generator, a 12 V battery, ignition, a kill switch and a pedal switch to start the engine when you press the accelerator.

## What a full conversion needs

| Part | Why you need it |
|---|---|
| Engine + mounting plate | Most swaps use a horizontal-shaft engine around 400–700 cc |
| Drive clutch, driven clutch and belt | Replace the direct motor drive |
| Gas-type rear end with F-N-R gearbox | Takes the driven clutch and gives you reverse |
| Fuel tank, lines, filter, pump | Gas carts use a pulse-driven [fuel pump](/shop/ezgo-golf-cart-fuel-pump-diagram-guide) |
| Throttle cable and pedal switch | Links the pedal to the carburettor and starts the engine on demand |
| 12 V battery, starter, wiring | Starting and ignition |
| Exhaust and heat shielding | Keeps heat away from the body and the seat |

A gas-to-gas swap is the useful benchmark. A Predator 420 engine plus a model-specific install kit typically costs **$675–$910 in parts**, and that's for a cart that already has a gas rear end, tank and wiring. An electric-to-gas build adds all of that on top.

## Better fixes for the usual reasons people want gas

People rarely want gas for its own sake. It's usually one of three things:

**"My range is bad."** Old lead-acid packs are the usual culprit. A 48 V lead-acid cart typically manages 15–25 miles, while a lithium pack of similar size often does 30–50 miles. Lead-acid batteries only give you about half their rated capacity before you start damaging them. A [lithium conversion](/blog/convert-golf-cart-to-lithium-battery) fixes range for far less work than a gas swap. Use our [golf cart range calculator](/shop/how-far-can-a-golf-cart-go) to see what your pack should be doing.

**"It struggles on hills."** On a series-wound electric cart, a higher-amp controller and heavier cables help a lot. Lithium also helps, because the voltage doesn't sag the way lead-acid does on a climb.

**"I don't want to charge it."** That's the one honest reason to go gas. In that case, sell the electric cart and buy a used gas cart. You'll get a factory-engineered drivetrain and a cart that's easier to resell.

## If you still want to do it

- **Use a donor cart.** The cleanest conversions take the complete rear end, engine and wiring from a wrecked gas cart of the same family.
- **Match parts to the year.** Mounting kits and clutches are specific to model and year. A kit for a 1994–2008 TXT won't fit an RXV.
- **Keep the governor sensible.** A bigger engine with the governor removed can push a cart well past what its brakes and steering were designed for. Our [governor guide](/shop/yamaha-golf-cart-governor-adjustment) explains the trade-offs.
- **Check your insurance and local rules.** A heavily modified cart can be harder to insure, and in some communities you can't register it for road use.

## Bottom line

An electric-to-gas conversion makes sense as a project you'll enjoy building. As a way to save money, it rarely does. If the real problem is range or hill power, fix the batteries or the controller first. If you simply want gas, buy a gas cart.
