Loft is the angle of the clubface relative to the shaft, and it decides most of how high and far each club sends the ball. Typical lofts are **9–12° for a driver, 15° for a 3-wood, 27–34° for a 7-iron, 41–47° for a pitching wedge and 54–58° for a sand wedge**. The exact numbers vary by model, and they have changed a lot in 20 years.

## Loft chart: woods and hybrids

| Club | Typical loft |
|---|---|
| Driver | 9–12° |
| 3-wood | 15–16° |
| 5-wood | 18–19° |
| 7-wood | 21–22° |
| 9-wood | 24–25° |
| 3-hybrid | 19–20° |
| 4-hybrid | 22–23° |
| 5-hybrid | 25–26° |

Fairway woods and hybrids overlap. A 7-wood and a 4-hybrid have almost the same loft, but they behave differently. See [fairway wood vs hybrid](/shop/fairway-wood-vs-hybrid).

## Loft chart: irons

Iron lofts depend heavily on the type of set:

| Club | Players' / traditional | Game-improvement |
|---|---|---|
| 4-iron | 22–24° | 18–21° |
| 5-iron | 25–27° | 21–24° |
| 6-iron | 28–31° | 24–27° |
| 7-iron | 32–34° | 27–31° |
| 8-iron | 36–38° | 31–35° |
| 9-iron | 40–42° | 37–40° |
| Pitching wedge | 44–47° | 41–44° |

## Loft chart: wedges and putter

| Club | Typical loft |
|---|---|
| Gap / approach wedge | 48–52° |
| Sand wedge | 54–58° |
| Lob wedge | 58–64° |
| Putter | 2–4° |

More detail on choosing wedge lofts is in our [wedge degrees guide](/shop/golf-wedge-degrees).

## Why your 7-iron isn't what it used to be

Over the past couple of decades, manufacturers have "strengthened" lofts. Lower centres of gravity and thinner, faster faces keep the ball launching high enough even with less loft, so a modern game-improvement 7-iron can have the loft an older 6-iron had. You hit it further, but:

- **The bottom of the set stretches.** A 42° pitching wedge followed by a 56° sand wedge leaves a 14° hole. That's roughly 25–30 yards with nothing in it. Most golfers need a gap wedge.
- **Comparing numbers across brands is misleading.** "My 7-iron goes 170" means little without the loft.

## How loft affects your shots

- **More loft** means a higher launch, more backspin and less roll. It also gives you more forgiveness on sidespin, which is why a slice is milder with a 9-iron than with a driver.
- **Less loft** means a lower, longer flight with more roll and more curve on mishits.
- **Driver loft is personal.** Slower swingers usually gain distance from more loft (10.5–12°), not less. See our [driver loft guide](/shop/best-loft-driver-golf).

## Check your own set

1. Look up your model's **spec sheet** on the manufacturer's site.
2. Write down every loft and look for gaps bigger than about 4–6°.
3. Use our [distance chart calculator](/shop/golf-club-distance-chart) to see where the yardage holes are.
4. Lofts can bend over time on forged irons, and a fitter can check and adjust them on a [loft and lie machine](/shop/best-golf-loft-lie-machine-guide).
