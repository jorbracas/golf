Titleist **discontinued the NXT line in 2018**. The **NXT Tour** and **NXT Tour S** were replaced by one ball, the **Tour Soft**, launched at **$35 a dozen**. Any NXTs you find now are old stock or recycled. Fine for practice, but you can't keep buying the same ball.

## What the NXT balls were

The NXT family sat below the Pro V1 and Pro V1x: a cheaper option for golfers who wanted Titleist quality without tour-ball spin or price.

- **NXT Tour:** the firmer of the two, aimed at distance with decent greenside control.
- **NXT Tour S:** the "S" stood for soft. It was a lower-compression version for golfers who prioritised feel, and it was also sold in yellow.

When Titleist retired them, it said its research showed golfers in this category mostly cared about two things, distance and feel, rather than construction details, so it replaced both NXTs with a single ball.

## What replaced them

| If you played… | Current Titleist option | Why |
|---|---|---|
| NXT Tour S (soft feel) | **Tour Soft** | Built around a very large 1.60-inch core and soft ionomer-blend cover for feel and distance |
| NXT Tour (distance) | **Tour Soft** or **Velocity** | Velocity is Titleist's distance-first ball. The 2018 version had a lower-compression core and came in several colours |
| NXT, but you want more short-game spin | **Pro V1 / AVX** | Urethane covers for more greenside spin, at a higher price |

## Are old or recycled NXTs worth it?

For **practice, scrambles and casual rounds**, good-condition recycled NXTs are a cheap way to play a decent ball. Keep in mind:

- They're at least **eight years old**. Stored badly, any ball loses some of its pop.
- Choose **"mint" or top-grade** recycled balls, and avoid scuffs and cuts.
- **Mixed batches play differently.** Tour and Tour S feel noticeably different.
