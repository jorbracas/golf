A one-person golf cart is usually a **single-rider golf scooter**: an electric two- or three-wheeler with a mount for your bag. Most cost **$2,000–$4,000**, run 36 holes or more on a lithium pack and weigh a fraction of a normal cart. The catch is that **not every course will let you use one**, so call the course before you buy.

If you mostly play one or two home courses that welcome them, a scooter is a fun, fast way to play solo. If you travel to different courses, a lightweight [walking bag](/shop/best-walking-golf-bags) and a push trolley may be the more practical choice.

## Types of single-rider carts

| Type | Typical price | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two-wheel golf scooter (e.g. Phat Scooters, Koppla) | ~$3,300–$3,400 | Solo golf, neighbourhood rides | Balance takes practice, lower weight limits |
| Golf "cycle" with centre-mounted bag (e.g. FinnCycle) | ~$3,500 | Lightweight, quick rounds | Often sold to courses as rental fleets |
| Three-wheel trike | ~$4,000 | Stability, older riders | Wider, slower top speeds |
| Board-style (GolfBoard) | ~$7,000 | Surf/skate feel | Learning curve, course approval |
| Budget scooters (e.g. Southbound) | ~$2,000 | Occasional use | Build quality, parts support |

Prices come from retailer listings at the time of writing and change often. Shipping can add around $400.

## What the specs mean

**Motor and voltage.** Most scooters use 1,000–2,000 W motors on 48–72 V packs. That's plenty for hills. Course models are often limited to 8–15 mph, and some let you pick a speed mode.

**Range.** Quoted figures run 25–50 miles. Two rounds a day is realistic on flat courses. Hills, heavier riders and cold mornings reduce range, as they do on a [full-size cart](/shop/how-far-can-a-golf-cart-go).

**Weight limit.** Typically 250–350 lbs including the rider. If you're near the limit, a trike is the safer choice.

**Turf impact.** Courses worry about tyre marks on wet ground. Wide, low-pressure tyres and a light machine (some cycles weigh under 100 lbs) help your case with the superintendent.

## Before you buy

1. **Ask your course first.** Ask whether they allow private single-rider carts, whether there's a trail fee, whether you need a waiver or proof of insurance, and whether they're restricted to paths after rain.
2. **Check where it can be serviced.** A scooter with no local parts supply becomes an ornament when a controller fails.
3. **Try one.** Many resorts rent single-rider carts. One round tells you whether you enjoy riding it.
4. **Think about storage and transport.** A scooter fits in an SUV or on a hitch rack. A standard cart needs a trailer.

## Pace of play

Single riders tend to play quickly: each player drives straight to their own ball. Some manufacturers pitch sub-two-hour rounds for a solo player. Our [round length guide](/shop/how-long-does-a-game-of-golf-last) shows how that compares with a typical foursome.
