A golf ball printer puts logos, names or photos onto the curved surface of a ball. There are two serious ways to do it: **UV inkjet printing** (full colour, no setup plates, good for short runs) and **pad printing** (cheap per ball on long runs of one or two colours). Both mean a real investment in equipment.

**If you need a few dozen personalised balls, don't buy a printer.** A printing service or the ball brand's own custom programme will be cheaper and better. A printer makes sense when you print week in, week out.

## UV vs pad printing

| | UV inkjet | Pad printing |
|---|---|---|
| How it works | Inkjet heads spray UV ink and a UV lamp cures it instantly. A jig holds a batch of balls | An etched plate holds ink, a silicone pad picks it up and presses it onto the ball |
| Colours | Full colour, gradients, photos | Usually 1–2 colours per pass |
| Setup per design | Just the file | A plate plus registration for each design |
| Best for | Short runs, one-offs, varied designs | Long runs of the same logo |
| Typical buyer | Pro shops, promo businesses, event companies | High-volume promo printers |

UV ink is formulated to flex and grip on curved surfaces, and benchtop UV printers with golf-ball jigs can print several balls per run. Industrial units handle much larger batches.

## What you need to start (UV)

- **A UV flatbed printer that can print on curved objects** at least ~43 mm (1.68 in) high, the diameter of a ball
- **A golf ball jig** that holds balls at a fixed height and spacing
- **Design software and a RIP**, plus a white ink channel if you print on coloured balls
- **Primer or adhesion promoter**, depending on the ball cover and ink system
- **Clean balls.** Fingerprints and mould release agents cause adhesion problems.

## Costs to think about

- **Equipment:** from a few thousand dollars for small benchtop UV machines to far more for industrial printers. Pad printers can be cheaper to buy but need plates for each design.
- **Consumables:** UV ink, primer, cleaning fluid, lamp and print-head maintenance.
- **Your time:** loading jigs, test prints, quality control.
- **Balls:** blanks or name-brand balls. If you add your logo to a branded ball, check the brand's policies before you sell them.

## Cheaper alternatives

- **Brand custom programmes:** most major ball makers offer logo and personalised balls, often with low minimums for text personalisation. See our [Callaway custom golf balls review](/shop/callaway-custom-golf-balls-review) for an example.
- **Local promo printers:** good for corporate days and tournament gifts.
- **DIY marking:** a self-inking ball stamp or a line-marking tool lets you mark your own balls for identification, which is all most golfers need. Our [ball marker guide](/shop/best-golf-ball-marker) covers the on-green kind.

## Bottom line

For a business with steady demand, a UV printer and a good jig give you flexibility and fast turnaround. For everyone else, ordering printed balls is cheaper, quicker and better quality.
