The governor on a Yamaha gas cart is a mechanical stop that limits engine RPM, and so top speed. On a G29/Drive you can usually raise the limit with **a 10 mm wrench and small adjustments to the governor rod's locknut**. Owners typically go from **about 12–13 mph to roughly 17 mph**. Push it further and you're asking the engine, clutches and brakes to do more than Yamaha designed them for.

Electric Yamahas don't have a governor at all: their speed is set in the controller. This guide only applies to gas carts.

## How a gas governor works

Inside the engine, flyweights spin with the crankshaft. As RPM rises they swing out and pull the throttle back through a linkage. On the outside, a **spring and rod** set how hard the governor can pull. More tension on the throttle side, or more rod exposed, lets the engine rev higher before the governor steps in. That gives you a higher top speed.

## Adjusting a G29 / Drive: the general steps

1. **Park on level ground**, engine off, parking brake on, key out.
2. **Remove the rear access panel** to reach the governor linkage near the engine.
3. **Find the governor rod and locknut.** It's the spring-loaded rod in the throttle linkage.
4. **Turn the nut a small amount** in the direction that exposes more rod. That raises the RPM limit.
5. **Test drive on a flat, safe stretch** with a GPS speed app, then repeat in small steps if needed.
6. **Stop around 17 mph** and make sure the cart idles and stops normally.

Model years differ in layout, and so does which direction counts as "more". If your cart doesn't match the description, follow the service manual for your model rather than guessing.

## Typical speeds

| Setting | Approximate top speed (flat ground) |
|---|---|
| Factory | 12–15 mph |
| Common governor adjustment | ~17 mph |
| Beyond that | Risk of over-revving; needs clutch/engine work to do safely |

## The risks that matter

- **Engine wear.** A small single-cylinder engine run constantly above its design RPM wears faster. Overtightening the adjustment can also lead to oil getting into the carburettor or muffler.
- **Brakes and handling.** Factory drum brakes and steering are designed for 12–15 mph. If you go faster, check the brakes, and [lift kits](/shop/best-golf-cart-lift-kits) with bigger tyres make it worse.
- **Warranty.** A modified governor can void engine warranty cover.
- **Rules and insurance.** Many communities and courses cap cart speeds. A faster cart on public roads needs to be a properly equipped LSV, not just an adjusted golf cart.

## Want more speed than that?

An adjusted governor is the cheapest few mph you'll get. Beyond it, the options are a [bigger engine swap](/shop/best-golf-cart-engine-swap), clutch and gearing changes or a different cart.
