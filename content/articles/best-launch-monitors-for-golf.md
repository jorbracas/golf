The right launch monitor depends on **two things: your budget and how deep your room is.** Roughly:

- **Under ~$1,000:** radar or hybrid units such as the **Garmin R10**, **Rapsodo MLM2PRO** or **Square Golf**. Great for practice and casual sim golf.
- **~$1,000–$3,000:** the **FlightScope Mevo** range, **Bushnell Launch Pro** and **Uneekor EYE MINI**. Much better indoor spin and club data.
- **$5,000+:** **Garmin R50**, **Full Swing KIT**, **Foresight GC3/GCQuad**, **Uneekor EYE XO2**, **TrackMan**. Near-tour data and premium simulator software.

**Camera units that sit beside the ball suit short rooms. Radar units that sit behind you need more depth.** Check your space with our [simulator room calculator](/shop/golf-simulator-room-size) first.

## Radar vs camera, in plain terms

**Radar (Doppler)** tracks the ball in flight. Outdoors, with room to fly, it's excellent. Indoors the ball only travels 10–12 ft before the screen, so the unit needs space behind you to see enough of the flight. Some budget units estimate spin, or need special marked balls to measure it.

**Camera (photometric)** units photograph the ball, and often the club, in the first few inches after impact. They work in shorter rooms and measure spin directly, which is why they're the default for serious indoor setups. The trade-off is price, and you need decent lighting around the hitting area.

**Overhead/ceiling** systems (Uneekor EYE XO2, TrackMan iO) keep the floor clear and work for left- and right-handers without moving anything. They need enough ceiling height and a fixed installation.

## The real cost: hardware + software + room

Many monitors need a **subscription** for full simulator play, course libraries or club data: typically **$100–$600 a year**. Add it up over five years before comparing two units on price. Then budget for the rest of the setup: screen, enclosure, mat and projector. Our [golf simulator cost guide](/shop/golf-simulator-cost) breaks it down.

## Which one should you buy?

- **Practice-focused, tight budget, garage with depth:** Garmin R10 or Rapsodo MLM2PRO. See our [launch monitors under $500](/shop/best-budget-golf-launch-monitor-under-500) for the cheapest options.
- **Short room (basement, spare room) and serious about data:** a side-of-ball camera unit such as the Bushnell Launch Pro or Uneekor EYE MINI.
- **Dedicated sim room, mixed left- and right-handers:** consider an overhead system.
- **"Buy once" premium:** Foresight GC3 or GCQuad, Garmin R50 (built-in display) or TrackMan.

Whatever you choose, **the launch monitor is the part to spend on first**. A great screen in front of an inaccurate monitor is still an inaccurate simulator. Our [simulator buying guide](/shop/best-golf-sim-buying-guide) puts all the pieces together.
