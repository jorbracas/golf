Yamaha's fuel-injected golf cart is the **Drive2 with the QuieTech EFI** gas powertrain. Instead of a carburettor, an electronic fuel-injection system meters fuel into the engine. For owners that means **easier cold starts, steadier running, fewer storage-related fuel problems and a noticeably quieter cart**. Yamaha lists the personal **Drive2 PTV QuieTech EFI at $9,899 MSRP**, and EFI Drive2s have been around since about 2017, so there's a healthy used market.

## EFI vs carburettor in plain terms

| | Carburettor (older Yamahas, e.g. G-series) | EFI (Drive2 QuieTech EFI) |
|---|---|---|
| How fuel is metered | Mechanically, by jets and airflow | Electronically, by sensors and an injector |
| Cold starts | Can need choke and patience | Usually start straight away |
| After long storage | Varnish can clog jets | Less prone to it, though old fuel still causes trouble |
| Running at altitude / in heat | Mixture can drift | Adjusts automatically |
| DIY tuning | Easy to tweak | Mostly dealer or electronic |
| Speed adjustment | [Mechanical governor](/shop/yamaha-golf-cart-governor-adjustment) | Electronic, not a simple rod adjustment |

## What "QuieTech" adds

QuieTech is Yamaha's name for its gas powertrain designed to cut engine noise and vibration. If you've only driven older gas carts, the difference in noise is the first thing you'll notice.

## Buying a used Yamaha EFI cart

1. **Check the year and model** from the serial plate. Drive2s from about 2017 on are the ones that can be EFI.
2. **Cold start it.** It should fire quickly and idle evenly without stalling.
3. **Look for warning lights or fault codes.** EFI faults show on the dash on many models. Ask whether the dealer has scanned it.
4. **Ask for the service history:** oil changes, air and fuel filters.
5. **Check the belt and clutches** under load on a hill. Slipping or juddering means maintenance is due.
6. **Compare with electric.** For mostly flat, short trips, a [Yamaha electric](/shop/yamaha-electric-golf-cart-review) with lithium may suit you better. Gas makes sense for long distances, hills and quick refuelling.

## Maintenance basics

- Change oil and filter at the intervals in your owner's manual
- Service the air filter more often on dusty roads
- Replace the fuel filter and spark plug as scheduled
- Use fresh fuel. Add stabiliser if the cart sits for more than a few weeks
- Leave EFI diagnostics to a dealer with the right tool

## Bottom line

If you want a gas cart, the Drive2 QuieTech EFI is one of the most refined you can buy: quieter, easier to live with and less fussy than carbureted carts. The trade-off is that you can't tinker with it as easily. Speed and fuelling changes are electronic, not a quick turn of a wrench.
