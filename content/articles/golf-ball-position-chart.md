The quick version: **driver just inside the lead heel**, **fairway woods and hybrids a ball's width or so inside that**, **long irons slightly forward of centre**, **mid and short irons in the centre**, and **wedges centre to one ball back**. The putter goes slightly forward of centre. The changes between clubs are small, a ball or two, not inches.

## Ball position chart

| Club | Ball position | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Driver | Just inside the lead heel | Catch it slightly on the upswing for launch and low spin |
| Fairway woods | About a ball's width inside the driver position | Sweep it at the bottom of the arc |
| Hybrids | Similar to fairway woods, or slightly further back | Shallow, slightly descending strike |
| Long irons (3–4) | Centre to slightly forward | Bottom of the arc just after the ball |
| Mid irons (5–7) | Centre | Ball first, then turf |
| Short irons (8–9) | Centre | Ball first, then turf |
| Full wedges | Centre to one ball back | Descending strike, lower flight |
| Chips | Around the middle, narrow stance | Clean contact with weight forward |
| Putter | Slightly forward of centre | Ball rolls rather than skids or hops |

"Centre" means the middle of your stance, measured from your feet, not from your nose.

## Why it changes from club to club

Your swing bottoms out at roughly the same spot every time, around or slightly ahead of your lead shoulder. With the driver, teed up, you want to catch the ball on the way up, so it goes furthest forward. Fairway woods sweep it at the bottom. Irons and wedges should meet the ball just before the bottom, so you strike ball then turf, which is why they sit further back. Moving the ball slightly forward or back is how you line those up.

## How stance width plays a part

- **Driver**: widest stance, about shoulder width or a little wider.
- **Irons**: a little narrower, shoulder width.
- **Wedges and chips**: narrower again.

As your stance narrows, keeping the ball "in the middle" naturally moves it a little closer to your trail foot. That's why wedges can look further back than they really are.

## Common ball position mistakes

| Mistake | Typical result |
|---|---|
| Driver too far back | Low, spinny or hooking drives |
| Driver too far forward | Pop-ups, pulls, or shoulders open at address |
| Fairway wood in the driver position | Thin or topped shots |
| Hybrid played like a long iron, too far back | Heavy, digging shots |
| Irons creeping forward over time | Fat shots and pulls |
| Wedges too far back | Very low flight, leading edge digs |

## How to check it

1. Lay one [alignment stick](/shop/best-alignment-sticks-for-golf-swing) along your toe line and a second at right angles pointing at the ball.
2. Film your setup face-on with your phone at hip height.
3. Recheck every few weeks. Ball position tends to drift without you noticing.
