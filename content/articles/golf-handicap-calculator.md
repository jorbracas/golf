Enter your recent rounds below with the **course rating** and **slope** from the scorecard (or the course's website). The calculator works out each round's score differential and estimates your **Handicap Index** using World Handicap System rules. It also shows your **course handicap** for the last course you entered.

## How the calculation works

**1. Score differential for each round**

> Differential = (Adjusted gross score − Course rating) × 113 ÷ Slope rating

113 is the slope of a course of "standard" difficulty. A harder course (higher slope) shrinks your differential, so a 95 on a tough course counts for more than a 95 on an easy one.

**2. Average your best differentials**

With 20 or more rounds, your Handicap Index is the **average of your lowest 8 of the last 20**. With fewer rounds, the WHS uses fewer differentials:

| Rounds in record | Differentials used | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Lowest 1 | −2.0 |
| 4 | Lowest 1 | −1.0 |
| 5 | Lowest 1 | 0 |
| 6 | Average of lowest 2 | −1.0 |
| 7–8 | Average of lowest 2 | 0 |
| 9–11 | Average of lowest 3 | 0 |
| 12–14 | Average of lowest 4 | 0 |
| 15–16 | Average of lowest 5 | 0 |
| 17–18 | Average of lowest 6 | 0 |
| 19 | Average of lowest 7 | 0 |
| 20 | Average of lowest 8 | 0 |

The maximum Handicap Index is **54.0**.

**3. Course handicap**

> Course handicap = Handicap Index × (Slope ÷ 113) + (Course rating − Par)

That's the number of strokes you receive on that course from those tees.

## What "adjusted gross score" means

Your handicap score isn't always your raw total. Under the WHS, the most you can count on any hole is **net double bogey**: par + 2 + any handicap strokes you get on that hole. A 10 on a par 4 where you get one stroke counts as a 7. New golfers without an index use par + 5 as the hole maximum.

## Why your official index may differ

Your national association (GHIN in the US, England Golf, Golf Australia, etc.) also applies a daily **playing conditions calculation**, soft and hard caps that limit how fast your index can rise, and exceptional-score reductions. This calculator ignores those, so treat its result as a good estimate. Your official index is the one to use in competitions.

The average Handicap Index in the US is **14.2 for men** and **28.7 for women** (USGA).
