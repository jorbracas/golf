A loft and lie machine clamps a clubhead so you can **measure and bend the hosel**, changing **loft** (how much the face points up) and **lie** (the shaft angle at address). It keeps distance gaps even and stops the face pointing left or right of your target at impact. **Forged irons bend easily and safely; cast irons are harder and riskier to adjust.** For a single set, a club fitter is cheaper than buying a machine.

## Why loft and lie matter

- **Lie too upright** (toe up at impact): the face points **left** for right-handed golfers, so shots start and curve left.
- **Lie too flat** (toe down): the face points **right**.
- **Loft drift:** over time, especially with soft forged irons, lofts can move, so your 8- and 9-iron end up going the same distance.

Impact tape on the sole or a lie board shows where the club contacts the ground.

## Forged vs cast

| | Forged (soft carbon steel) | Cast (harder alloys) |
|---|---|---|
| Ease of bending | Easier, needs less force | Harder, needs more force |
| Typical adjustment room | Generous | Smaller and riskier |
| Risk of cracking | Low with proper technique | Higher; some heads shouldn't be bent |
| Tooling note | Brass sole rests and bars protect the finish | Mitchell advises **not** using a brass bar on cast heads, since cast is harder than brass |

Multi-material and hollow-body irons often have limited or no adjustment. Check the manufacturer's guidance before you bend.

## How bending is done

1. **Measure first** to get a starting reference for loft and lie.
2. **Clamp the head** with protective pads and position the bending bar on the hosel.
3. **Bend in small steps**, then re-measure. Adjusting lie can change loft accidentally (and vice versa), so check both after every bend.
4. **Recheck the gaps** across the set so the distances stay even.

## Home machine vs club fitter

| | Home machine | Club fitter |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | From a few hundred dollars (hobby models) to thousands (professional) | Usually a modest fee per club or set |
| Skill needed | Real learning curve | None for you |
| Best for | Club builders, people who tinker, clubs for several golfers | Most golfers |

Forums are full of hobbyists debating budget versus professional machines: the pro models are more rigid and repeatable, which matters when you adjust many clubs. If you're only checking your own irons once a season, a fitter is the better buy.

## Before any bending

- Know your **swing data**: a lie check on a lie board or with impact tape beats guessing from a static measurement.
- Make sure **length and grip size** are right first. They affect lie at impact too. See [standard iron lengths](/shop/standard-length-of-golf-irons).
- Keep a **spec sheet** of your before and after numbers.
