The fastest golf cart on record is **"Bandit"**, built by Plum Quick Motors. It hit **118.76 mph** at Darlington Dragway in South Carolina in 2014 and set the Guinness World Record. It looks like a golf cart, but it was a purpose-built drag machine.

The cart in your garage is a different story. Most golf carts are governed to **12–15 mph**, and street-legal low-speed vehicles top out at **25 mph** by law.

## How fast real carts go

| Type of cart | Typical top speed |
|---|---|
| Course fleet cart | 12–15 mph (often set lower by the course) |
| Personal gas cart, factory | 12–15 mph |
| Gas cart with governor adjusted | ~17 mph on many models |
| Lithium personal cart, sport mode | 18–20 mph on many current models |
| Street-legal LSV | 25 mph maximum (e.g. Club Car Onward LSV) |
| Modified "speed" carts | 25–40+ mph (not road-legal as golf carts) |

## Where extra speed comes from

**Gas carts:** a [governor adjustment](/shop/yamaha-golf-cart-governor-adjustment) is the cheap first step. Beyond that you're looking at clutch springs, gearing or a bigger engine swap.

**Electric carts:** top speed depends on the motor's design RPM, the controller's current limit and the pack voltage. A "high-speed" motor with a matched controller is the usual upgrade. Lithium batteries help because their voltage doesn't sag under load the way lead-acid does. Our [guide to making a cart faster](/blog/how-to-make-a-golf-cart-fast) goes through the options in order of cost.

**Tyres:** taller tyres raise top speed a little and cost you torque. That's why a [lifted cart](/shop/best-golf-cart-lift-kits) on 22-inch tyres can feel slower up hills even when the speedometer shows more on the flat.

## The part speed videos skip

A standard cart has small drum brakes, a short wheelbase, no crumple zones and often no seat belts. At 25 mph it covers more than 36 ft every second. Before you add speed:

- Upgrade to front disc brakes if your model supports them.
- Fit seat belts and keep passengers seated.
- Check steering components and alignment, especially after a lift.
- Know your local rules. A cart doing more than 20 mph on public roads usually has to be a properly equipped and registered [LSV](/shop/best-low-speed-vehicle-golf-cart), not just a fast golf cart.

## Bottom line

118 mph makes a good headline. For your own cart, 17–20 mph with good brakes is the practical sweet spot, and 25 mph belongs on a cart built and registered as an LSV.
