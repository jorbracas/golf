**Fairway woods** are longer, with a bigger, flatter head. They're best off the tee and from clean fairway lies, and they're the easier club for many slower swingers to get airborne. **Hybrids** are shorter and more iron-like, better from the **rough, uneven lies and tight spots**. Most amateurs benefit from carrying **both** in place of their long irons.

## The key differences

| | Fairway wood | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|
| Typical lofts | 15–25° (3-, 5-, 7-, 9-wood) | 18–28° (2- to 6-hybrid) |
| Head size | ~160–240 cc, shallow and wide | ~80–120 cc, compact |
| Shaft | Longer | Shorter, closer to an iron |
| Lie angle | Flatter | More upright |
| Best from | Tee, fairway, flat lies | Rough, slopes, fairway bunkers, chip-and-runs |
| How to hit it | Sweep it off the turf | Slightly descending blow, like an iron |

The bigger head of a fairway wood gives a larger sweet spot and more distance. The hybrid's compact head and sharper leading edge cut through grass far better, which is why it's the go-to from the rough.

## 7-wood vs 4-hybrid

This is the most common head-to-head, because the two clubs share almost the same loft: about **21–22° for a 7-wood** and **22–23° for a 4-hybrid**.

- **Distance**: the 7-wood often carries further. In a GOLFTEC test it flew around 20 yards past the 4-hybrid for both testers, helped by its longer shaft and bigger head.
- **Flight**: the 7-wood launches higher and lands softer, which is great for holding greens with long approaches.
- **From the rough**: the 4-hybrid wins. The 7-wood's bigger head gets snagged in thick grass.
- **Off the tee**: the 7-wood is easier to hit high and straight from a tee peg.
- **Feel**: hybrids suit golfers who like to hit down on the ball like an iron; 7-woods suit sweepers.

If you play a lot of parkland golf with thick rough, take the hybrid. If you play firm, open courses and struggle to stop long irons on greens, try the 7-wood.

## Which suits your game?

Golf Insider UK's PGA professional Will Shaw breaks it down by level:

- **Low handicappers**: largely preference. A 3- or 5-wood for tee shots and par-5s; faster swingers may still prefer long irons for control.
- **Mid handicappers**: a mix of both, and consider removing the 3- and 4-iron.
- **High handicappers**: replace long irons, often down to the 5- or 6-iron, with fairway woods and hybrids. Slower swingers often get more distance from fairway woods; faster swingers may prefer hybrids for control.

## Building your set

1. Check your **lofts**, not the numbers on the sole. Our [loft chart](/shop/golf-club-loft-chart) shows typical values.
2. Aim for about **4° between long clubs** so each one has its own distance.
3. Check the gaps with our [distance calculator](/shop/golf-club-distance-chart).
4. A common forgiving setup: driver, 3-wood or 5-wood, 7-wood or 4-hybrid, 5-hybrid, then irons from 6 down. For more help, see [forgiving irons](/shop/best-forgiving-golf-irons-guide).
