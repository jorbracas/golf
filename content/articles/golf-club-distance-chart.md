Most amateur men hit a **driver about 205–235 yards**, a **7-iron about 130–150 yards** and a **pitching wedge about 100–120 yards**. Tour pros carry the driver around 270 yards and a 7-iron around 170. Below are the real averages from USGA and TrackMan data, and a calculator that builds a chart from your own swing speed.

## How far amateurs actually hit it

In 2023 the USGA measured 627 recreational golfers (548 men and 79 women) hitting real shots. These are the average distances (median of seven shots per club):

| Club | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Driver | 226 yd | 144 yd |
| 7-iron | 149 yd | 98 yd |
| Pitching wedge | 117 yd | 80 yd |

Driver distance tracks handicap closely. The study found men gain about **30 yards for every 10 shots of handicap** and women about 23:

| Handicap Index | Men's driver | Women's driver |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 235 yd | 177 yd |
| 20 | 205 yd | 154 yd |
| 30 | 175 yd | 131 yd |

These are total distances, carry plus roll. If those numbers look shorter than you expected, you're not alone. The same USGA study found golfers tend to **overestimate** how far they hit their longer clubs.

## Tour pros: the TrackMan averages

For context, these are TrackMan **carry** averages for the PGA Tour and LPGA, as published by GolfWRX (2013 data; today's tour players are a little longer):

| Club | PGA Tour carry | LPGA carry |
|---|---|---|
| Driver | 269 yd | 220 yd |
| 3-wood | 243 yd | 195 yd |
| 5-wood | 230 yd | 185 yd |
| Hybrid | 225 yd | – |
| 4-iron | 203 yd | 170 yd |
| 5-iron | 194 yd | 161 yd |
| 6-iron | 183 yd | 152 yd |
| 7-iron | 172 yd | 141 yd |
| 8-iron | 160 yd | 130 yd |
| 9-iron | 148 yd | 119 yd |
| Pitching wedge | 136 yd | 107 yd |

According to TrackMan, PGA Tour players now average around 115 mph of driver club speed, while the average male amateur is around 94 mph. That's why their numbers are so different.

## Build your own chart

[[tool]]

The calculator scales the tour ratios between clubs to your speed. To get your inputs:

- **Swing speed**: a [swing speed radar](/shop/best-golf-swing-speed-tracker) or any launch monitor will tell you.
- **7-iron carry**: hit ten balls at a range with clear yardage markers, ignore the worst two and the best two, and average the rest.

## How to use your numbers

1. **Know your carry, not your best shot.** Plan around your average. Most amateur misses are short, not long.
2. **Check your gaps.** Aim for **10–15 yards between irons**. If your 4- and 5-iron go the same distance, a [hybrid](/shop/fairway-wood-vs-hybrid) may serve you better.
3. **Check your lofts.** Modern irons are stronger-lofted than older sets, so a "7-iron" today can be an old 6-iron. Our [loft chart](/shop/golf-club-loft-chart) explains why.
4. **Mind the wedges.** The biggest gaps usually sit between the pitching wedge and sand wedge. See our [wedge degrees guide](/shop/golf-wedge-degrees).

## What changes your distances

- **Strike quality**: off-centre hits lose ball speed, so the same swing can fly well short of your average.
- **Shaft and fit**: the wrong [shaft flex](/shop/golf-shaft-flex-chart) costs consistency more than distance.
- **Conditions**: cold air, wind, altitude and firm or soft ground all move the numbers.
- **The ball**: a [low-spin ball](/shop/best-low-spin-golf-balls) can add a few yards with the driver for higher-spin players.
