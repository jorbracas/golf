An impact screen is the surface you hit into and project onto, so it affects **picture quality, noise, safety and lifespan**. For most home sims the best value is a **premium three-layer screen in grey**, installed with **bungees, a gap behind it, and 10–12 ft from the tee**. The installation matters as much as the material.

## Screen material tiers

| Tier | Construction | Picture | Bounce-back | Typical price | Typical lifespan (regular use) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Entry** | Open-weave single-layer polyester | Acceptable, grainy | Moderate to high | ~$180–$400 | 1–2 years |
| **Mid** | Tight-weave reinforced polyester | Good HD | Low to moderate | ~$280–$600 | 2–3 years |
| **Premium** | 3-layer: two polyester faces + spacer yarn | Excellent, 4K-capable | Very low | ~$460–$1,060+ | 3–5+ years |

The premium screen's sandwich construction cushions the ball. It's quieter, and a properly tensioned premium screen typically sends the ball back only a foot or three.

## White vs grey

- **Grey** absorbs more ambient light, roughly 40% more by one test, so you get **better contrast and less washout** in garages with windows or overhead lights. It usually costs only $10–$15 more.
- **White** is brighter in a fully blacked-out room or with a lower-lumen projector.

If in doubt, grey is the safer pick for most garages and basements.

## Installation rules that matter

1. **Leave a gap behind the screen.** At least ~12 in for a tensioned screen and up to ~20 in for a hanging one. A screen tight against a wall can't absorb impact.
2. **Tension with bungees, not rigid fixings.** The screen should flex a few inches when you push it by hand.
3. **Tee 10–12 ft from the screen.** Closer increases the risk of balls bouncing back.
4. **Use side and top netting** (an enclosure) to catch mishits. See our [enclosure guide](/shop/best-golf-enclosure).
5. **Hit clean balls.** Dirt, grass and marker ink transfer onto the screen at impact speed and permanently dull the picture.

## Sizing the screen

Match the screen to your room and projector, not the other way round. Most home screens are roughly 10–12 ft wide. The aspect ratio (4:3 vs 16:9/16:10) should match your projector to avoid black bars or a picture that spills over the edges. Our [screen and projector guide](/shop/best-golf-screen-and-projector) explains how to pair them, and the [room calculator](/shop/golf-simulator-room-size) gives you the space limits.

## Bottom line

Spend on a premium grey screen if you'll use the sim several times a week. It's quieter and safer and will outlast two cheaper screens. If you're testing the waters with a net and a TV, skip the screen until you know you'll use a full simulator.
