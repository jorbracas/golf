A **pitching wedge is usually 41–47°**. Beyond that, a **gap wedge is 48–52°**, a **sand wedge 54–58°** and a **lob wedge 58–64°**. The key isn't the exact numbers but the spacing: wedges about **4–6° apart** give you even distance gaps of roughly 10–15 yards.

## Wedge loft chart

| Wedge | Typical loft | Most common |
|---|---|---|
| Pitching wedge (PW) | 41–47° | 44–46° |
| Gap / approach wedge (GW, AW, UW) | 48–52° | 50° or 52° |
| Sand wedge (SW) | 54–58° | 56° |
| Lob wedge (LW) | 58–64° | 60° |

Why such a wide range for the pitching wedge? Iron lofts have got stronger over the past 20 years. **Game-improvement sets often have a 41–43° PW**, while **traditional players' irons sit at 45–46°**. Our [loft chart](/shop/golf-club-loft-chart) shows the whole set.

## How far does each wedge go?

The USGA's study of recreational golfers puts the average full **pitching wedge at about 117 yards for men and 80 yards for women**. Tour players carry it around 136. Each step down in wedge loft typically costs 10–15 yards on a full swing. Built from those averages, these are approximate full-swing ranges:

| Wedge | Typical male amateur | Typical female amateur |
|---|---|---|
| Pitching wedge | 105–125 yd | 70–90 yd |
| Gap wedge | 90–110 yd | 60–80 yd |
| Sand wedge | 75–95 yd | 50–70 yd |
| Lob wedge | 55–80 yd | 40–60 yd |

Treat these as ranges, not targets. Your own numbers come from your swing speed; the [club distance calculator](/shop/golf-club-distance-chart) estimates them.

## Choosing wedge lofts that leave no gaps

1. **Start from your pitching wedge loft.** Look it up on the spec sheet, don't guess.
2. **Add wedges every 4–6°.** A 44° PW pairs well with 50° and 56° wedges, or with 48°, 52°, 56° and 60° if you carry four.
3. **Watch the big hole.** A strong 42° PW followed by a 56° sand wedge leaves 14°, which is a full club or more with no wedge in it. That's the most common gap in amateur bags.
4. **Make room in the bag.** Fourteen clubs is the limit, so an extra wedge often replaces a long iron you rarely hit well.

## What about bounce?

Bounce is the angle between the leading edge and the lowest point of the sole. Titleist's Vokey team classes **low bounce as 4–6°, mid as 7–10° and high as over 10°**. More bounce stops the club digging in soft sand and turf; less bounce suits firm ground and players who like to open the face. For most amateurs, a sand wedge with around 10–12° of bounce is a safe all-round choice.

## Which wedge for which shot?

- **Pitching wedge**: full shots, low running chips. See [how to chip](/blog/how-to-chip-in-golf).
- **Gap wedge**: full shots between PW and SW distances, longer pitches.
- **Sand wedge**: bunkers, standard pitches, soft chips.
- **Lob wedge**: high, short shots over trouble. It's a specialist club. Our [60° wedge guide](/shop/best-60-wedge-golf-guide) covers when it's worth carrying.
