For most neighbourhood carts, a **4–6 inch spindle or A-arm lift** with **20–22 inch tyres** is the sweet spot. It gives you the look, more ground clearance and a ride that's still close to stock. Budget **$800–$2,000 all in** for the kit, wheels, tyres and installation. Long-travel kits and 23–25 inch tyres are for carts that actually go off pavement.

## Lift kit types compared

| Kit type | Lift | Max tyre size (approx.) | Track widening | Cost (kit only) | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Block** | small | ~20 in | none | lowest | Mild clearance, budget |
| **Spindle / drop axle** | 3–6 in | ~20–23 in | up to ~4 in | $150–$400 | Neighbourhood carts, easy DIY |
| **A-arm** | 4 or 6 in | ~20 in (4 in) / ~22 in (6 in) | up to ~2 in | $400–$900 | Better ride, light trails |
| **Long travel** | 4–8+ in, adjustable | ~25 in | up to ~8 in | $1,000+ | Real off-road use |

Figures are typical. Always check the maximum tyre size for your exact kit and cart.

## Spindle vs A-arm in plain terms

A **spindle kit** swaps the front spindles (or the axle) for taller ones and adds rear lift blocks. It's cheap, quick to bolt on and keeps the factory ride feel. It's the right answer for most carts that live on paths and roads.

An **A-arm kit** replaces the front control arms, which adds suspension travel. The ride is smoother over bumps and it takes bigger tyres on a 6-inch lift. It's worth the money if your cart sees gravel, grass or a farm track.

**Long travel** is a different class: fully independent front suspension and much wider stance. For a cart that never leaves the cul-de-sac, it's overkill.

## Matching tyres and wheels

| Lift | Tyre height | Common wheel size |
|---|---|---|
| ~3 in | up to ~20 in | 10–14 in |
| 4–5 in | ~20–22 in | 10–14 in |
| 6 in | up to ~23 in | 12–14 in |

A common pairing is a 6-inch lift with 12- or 14-inch wheels and 22-inch tyres. Our [wheel size guide](/shop/best-golf-cart-wheel-size) and [205/50-10 tyre guide](/shop/best-205-50x10-golf-cart-tires-buyers-guide) cover the details. Remember that offset wheels add about an inch of width.

## The downsides nobody puts in the listing

- **Stability:** a higher centre of gravity means more body roll. Take corners slower, especially with passengers in the back.
- **Torque and hills:** bigger tyres act like taller gearing, so acceleration and hill climbing suffer. Electric carts may want a higher-amp controller.
- **Braking:** heavier wheels and tyres lengthen stopping distances.
- **Range:** expect electric range to drop somewhat. Our [range calculator](/shop/how-far-can-a-golf-cart-go) includes a lift-kit option.
- **Width and height:** a lifted cart may no longer fit your trailer, gate or garage door. Check the numbers in our [dimensions guide](/shop/golf-cart-dimensions).
- **Alignment:** have the front end aligned after installation. Toe-in that's off wears tyres quickly and makes the steering wander.

## Before you buy

1. **Match the kit to your exact model and year.** Club Car DS and Precedent, and E-Z-GO TXT and RXV, all take different kits.
2. **Electric or gas?** Some kits are specific to the drivetrain.
3. **Plan the whole package:** kit, wheels, tyres, possibly a steering stabilizer and alignment.
4. **Consider the body.** Lifted carts are often paired with a new body, such as a [Bronco-style kit](/shop/golf-cart-body-kits-bronco).
