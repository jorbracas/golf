The best iron shaft is the one that matches your **swing speed, tempo and body**. As a rule: **heavier steel (about 110–130 g)** suits fast, aggressive swings, **lightweight steel (~94–104 g)** suits most average players, and **graphite (about 50–125 g)** suits slower swings and anyone with sore elbows or wrists. Heavier graphite now plays very like steel. Test before you reshaft a whole set.

## Steel, lightweight steel, graphite

| Type | Typical weight | Good for | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard steel** | ~110–130 g | 6-iron speeds ~80+ mph, quick tempo, control | Firm, direct feedback |
| **Lightweight steel** | ~94–104 g | Average speeds wanting a little more speed and launch | Livelier than heavy steel |
| **Graphite** | ~50–125 g | Slower speeds, joint pain, higher launch | Damped vibration |

An independent shaft test reported a heavy graphite shaft producing slightly *faster* ball speed than the heavy-steel average, which busts the old "graphite is short and wild" myth for many players.

## Flex and swing speed

| 6-iron clubhead speed | Usual starting point |
|---|---|
| Under ~70 mph | Light graphite, senior/regular |
| ~70–80 mph | Regular, lightweight steel or mid-weight graphite |
| ~80–90 mph | Stiff steel or heavier graphite |
| 90+ mph | Stiff / X-stiff, heavier steel |

Tempo changes the answer. A smooth swinger at 85 mph may love a softer shaft, while an aggressive 78-mph swinger may need something firmer. A [swing speed radar](/shop/best-golf-swing-speed-tracker) gets you the number for a fraction of a fitting's cost. Our [stiff vs regular guide](/shop/stiff-vs-regular-golf-shaft) explains how flex feels.

## Popular iron shaft families

- **True Temper Dynamic Gold:** classic heavy steel, low launch, widely played on tour (the S300 is a staple).
- **KBS Tour:** steel with a smoother feel, available in several weights and flexes.
- **Nippon N.S. Pro Modus3:** lighter-to-mid weight steel that many players find easy to swing.
- **Project X LZ:** steel designed for a stable but responsive load profile.
- **Graphite options** from UST, Fujikura and Mitsubishi: lightweight for speed, heavy models for steel-like control.

## Before you reshaft

1. **Test one club first.** A single 7-iron test is roughly **$80–$130**. A full set reshaft is roughly **$500–$900**.
2. **Get fitted** (about **$49–$175**) on a launch monitor. Look at dispersion and spin, not just distance.
3. **Check loft and lie afterwards.** New shafts and lengths can change how the club sits. See our [loft and lie guide](/shop/best-golf-loft-lie-machine-guide).
4. **Keep weights consistent** through the set, with wedges similar or slightly heavier.

## Bottom line

If your elbows hurt or your irons fly low and short, try graphite or lightweight steel. If you swing hard and the ball balloons or goes left, try a heavier, firmer steel shaft. Either way, a 30-minute test on a launch monitor will tell you more than any chart.
