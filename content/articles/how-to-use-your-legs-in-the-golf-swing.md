Your legs power the golf swing by **pushing against the ground**. In simple terms: **load into the trail side in the backswing, shift pressure toward the lead foot as you start down, then firm up ("post") the lead leg through impact** so your hips can keep turning. Get that sequence right and you'll find speed and solid contact without swinging harder with your arms.

## What the research says

Biomechanics studies measure ground reaction forces under each foot. A study of average golfers found that **the lead leg generally carries greater force** during the swing, and that **peak force under the trail leg comes earlier** than under the lead leg. That reflects the shift from back foot to front foot that starts late in the backswing. Other research links better use of the ground with greater clubhead speed.

## The sequence, step by step

1. **Address:** athletic flex in both knees, weight roughly even, pressure in the middle of the feet. Our [setup and posture guide](/blog/golf-setup-posture) covers the basics.
2. **Backswing:** turn your hips and chest while the **trail knee keeps its flex**. Feel pressure build in the inside of the trail foot, not the outside edge.
3. **Transition:** before the arms come down, **pressure starts moving toward the lead foot**. Many coaches describe it as a small bump or shift of the lead hip toward the target.
4. **Downswing to impact:** push into the ground and let the **lead leg firm up**. This "post" stops the lower body sliding and turns that energy into rotation.
5. **Finish:** most of your weight on the lead foot, trail heel up, belt buckle facing the target.

## Common leg mistakes

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Sway** | Hips drift away from the target in the backswing | Keep pressure on the inside of the trail foot and turn. See our [sway guide](/blog/how-to-stop-swaying-in-the-golf-swing) |
| **Slide** | Hips drift toward the target without turning in the downswing | Feel the lead leg "post" and the lead hip turn back behind you |
| **Straightening the trail leg early** | Loses posture and turn | Keep the trail knee flexed until the top of the swing |
| **Dead legs** | Arms-only swing, little speed | Practise feeling the shift and push from the ground |

## Four drills

1. **Step drill:** start with your feet together, step the lead foot toward the target as you start the downswing, then swing through. It teaches the pressure shift.
2. **Alignment stick by the trail hip:** place a stick just outside your trail hip. In the backswing, turn without bumping it to stop the sway.
3. **Lead-leg post drill:** make half swings and feel the lead leg straighten just after impact while the hips keep turning.
4. **Pause at the top:** pause for a second at the top, then start down with pressure moving to the lead foot before the arms.

Measure progress with a [swing speed radar](/shop/best-golf-swing-speed-tracker). Better leg action usually shows up as more speed for the same effort. For more ideas, see [how to get more distance](/blog/how-to-get-more-distance-in-golf).

If you have knee, hip or back problems, ease into these movements and check with a physio or certified instructor before adding speed training.
