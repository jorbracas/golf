A Bronco-style body kit replaces your cart's front cowl and rear body with panels shaped like a classic SUV: a squared nose, round headlights, flat fenders and a boxy rear. Most kits cost **about $750–$2,000**. You fit them with hand tools in a day or two, **as long as the kit is made for your exact cart model and year**.

Bronco kits don't make a cart faster or more capable. They change the look, and they usually go hand in hand with a lift kit and bigger tyres.

## Which carts they fit

Body kits are built for a specific frame, so check fitment before anything else:

| Brand | Common platforms for aftermarket bodies |
|---|---|
| Club Car | DS (1984+), Precedent, Onward, Tempo |
| E-Z-GO | TXT (1994+), RXV (2008+) |
| Yamaha | Drive (G29), Drive2 |

If you're not sure which E-Z-GO you have, our guide to [telling what year an EZGO is](/blog/how-to-tell-what-year-a-ezgo-golf-cart-is) walks through the serial number. Club Car and Yamaha also put a serial plate on the frame.

## ABS vs fiberglass

**ABS plastic** is what most bolt-on kits use. It flexes on impact rather than cracking, and the colour usually runs through the material, so light scratches buff out. It's the practical choice for a cart that gets used.

**Fiberglass** shows up on boutique retro and Bronco-style bodies. It looks more like a car and holds sharp lines, but it chips, and repairs mean gel coat and paint. Expect a longer install, sometimes with trimming.

## What's usually in the box

- Front cowl and rear body panels
- Grille
- Mounting hardware and instructions
- On "full" kits, an LED light kit with headlights, tail lights and often turn signals

Seats, roof, wheels, lift and paint are usually separate purchases. If the photos show a lifted cart on 22-inch tyres, that's another $800–$2,000 of parts. Our [lift kit guide](/shop/best-golf-cart-lift-kits) breaks that down.

## Before you order

1. **Confirm the model and year range** against your serial number, not by eye.
2. **Look for a light kit**, or budget for one. Bronco bodies look odd without round headlights.
3. **Check freight costs and inspect on delivery.** Panels ship on pallets, and damage claims are easier if you note them on the delivery receipt.
4. **Think about resale.** A tasteful body sells well in golf cart communities. A very loud one can narrow your buyers.
5. **Check community rules.** Some HOAs and 55+ communities limit modifications or lighting colours.

## Alternatives

If the Bronco look is too much, there are truck-style rear bodies ([our truck body kit guide](/shop/golf-cart-truck-body-kits)) and [Jeep-style bodies](/shop/best-jeep-body-for-golf-cart). Or you can keep the factory body and add a lift, wheels and a colour-matched seat set, which often makes more difference per dollar.
