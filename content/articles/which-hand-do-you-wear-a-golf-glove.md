You wear a golf glove on your **lead hand**, the one at the top of the grip. For a **right-handed golfer that's the left hand**, and for a **left-handed golfer it's the right hand**. When you shop, "left-hand glove" means a glove worn *on* the left hand, which is the one right-handed players buy.

## Why the lead hand

Your lead hand holds the top of the club and controls the clubface. Most of the grip pressure, and most of the slipping, happens there, especially in heat, humidity or rain. A glove gives you a consistent tacky surface and stops blisters on the heel pad and fingers. The trail hand is usually left bare so you can feel the club.

## Two gloves, no glove

- **Two gloves:** perfectly legal and common in rain, cold weather, or for golfers with sensitive skin or arthritis. Rain gloves are often sold in pairs and grip better when wet.
- **No glove:** some good players never wear one. If your grips are in good condition and you don't blister, it's your choice.
- **Putting:** most golfers take the glove off to putt and for delicate chips, to get more feel.

## Getting the fit right

1. **Snug, not tight.** There should be no loose leather at the fingertips or across the palm.
2. **The tab should fasten about three-quarters across.** If it overlaps fully, the glove is too big. If it barely reaches, it's too small.
3. **Regular vs cadet.** Cadet sizes have shorter fingers and a wider palm. If regular gloves always leave spare material at your fingertips, try cadet. Our [cadet vs regular guide](/shop/cadet-vs-regular-golf-glove) explains it.
4. **Leather vs synthetic.** Cabretta leather feels best but wears faster. Synthetic and hybrid gloves last longer and handle sweat better.

## Make it last

- Take it off between shots on hot days and fasten it to your bag.
- Flatten it after the round and keep it in its packet or a glove holder so it doesn't dry out stiff.
- Rotate two gloves during a round in the heat.
- Carry a spare. It's on our [golf bag checklist](/shop/golf-bag-checklist) for a reason.

A glove that wears through quickly at the heel pad can be a sign of a grip held too much in the palm, or grips that need replacing.
