When a golf cart charger won't start, the cause is usually something simple: **the outlet, a pack so flat the charger can't detect it, a blown fuse or a corroded receptacle.** Work through the steps below in order. Most fixes cost nothing or a few dollars, so check everything before you replace a $300–$600 charger.

Use a multimeter, work with the cart switched off and keep metal tools away from battery terminals.

## Step 1: power at the wall

- Plug a lamp or phone charger into the same outlet. Does it work?
- Reset any tripped **GFCI** outlet or breaker.
- Avoid long or thin extension cords. They can drop the voltage enough to stop the charger.

## Step 2: is the pack too flat?

Most chargers need to "see" a minimum pack voltage before they start. On a **48 V system that's roughly 30 V**. Measure across the main pack terminals:

- **Below the threshold:** the charger thinks no battery is connected. See our [guide to charging a dead golf cart battery](/blog/how-to-charge-a-dead-golf-cart-battery) for safe ways to bring it back.
- **Normal voltage but still no charge:** carry on.

A pack that keeps falling this low usually has a [failing battery](/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-golf-cart-battery-is-bad), or something on the cart drains it in storage.

## Step 3: fuses, receptacle and cord

- **Cart-side fuse:** many carts have a fuse between the charging receptacle and the pack. A blown fuse stops charging even though everything else looks fine.
- **Receptacle:** look for burnt, loose or corroded pins. Heat damage here is common and worth fixing quickly.
- **DC cord:** check the charger's output cord for cuts and test it for continuity.

## Step 4: brand-specific checks

**E-Z-GO (PowerWise / Delta-Q based chargers).** Watch the LED when you plug in. Common flash codes include **1 = battery voltage too high, 2 = too low, 3 = charge timeout, 4 = battery temperature too high, 6 = internal fault**. A timeout often points to a weak battery rather than a bad charger.

**Club Car (OBC carts, roughly 1995–2014).** These use an on-board computer that controls charging. It can sometimes be reset by disconnecting the main negative battery cable with the cart in neutral, waiting, then reconnecting. Follow the exact steps in your manual, as procedures differ by model.

**Yamaha.** Listen for a relay **click a few seconds after plugging in**. No click, with good voltage and a working outlet, suggests a relay or control-board problem in the charger or cart.

## Step 5: charger or batteries?

- The charger starts and then **stops after a few minutes:** often a bad battery or a poor connection. Test each battery.
- It **runs forever** without finishing: a battery may have a failed cell, or the charger isn't regulating.
- **Nothing at all** after all the checks above: the charger is the likely culprit. Get it bench-tested if a local shop offers it.

## Switching to lithium?

Lead-acid chargers aren't a safe match for most lithium packs. Use a [lithium-compatible charger](/shop/best-golf-cart-lithium-battery-charger) with the correct profile. Our [golf cart charger guide](/shop/best-golf-cart-battery-charger) covers what to buy.
