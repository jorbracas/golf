HZRDUS is Project X's line of graphite driver and wood shafts, known for **stable, low-torque profiles that suit faster, aggressive swings**. Pick the model by the **launch and spin you need**, and pick the flex code by **swing speed**. A 6.0 HZRDUS plays firmer than many brands' "stiff".

If your driver speed is under about 90 mph, most HZRDUS models will feel boardy. Look at the Red versions or a lighter shaft from another line.

## Current HZRDUS models at a glance

| Model | Launch / spin | Weights | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HZRDUS Black (Gen 5)** | Low / low | 60, 70 g | Fast, aggressive swings (~105+ mph) fighting spin |
| **Smoke Black RDX** | Low-mid / low | 50, 60, 70 g | Strong players who want a slightly smoother feel |
| **Smoke Blue RDX** | Mid / mid-low | 60, 70 g | The all-rounder, widest fitting window |
| **Silver (Gen 4)** | Mid / mid-low | 50–70 g | Balanced profile, a good fitting starting point |
| **Smoke Red RDX** | Mid-high / mid | 60, 70, 80 g | ~90–105 mph players who need launch |
| **Red CB (Gen 4)** | Mid-high / mid-high | 40–60 g | Moderate speeds, higher flight |
| **Smoke IM10** | Mid-low / low | 50, 60, 70 g | Premium carbon version, low spin |

Older models (the original Black, Yellow and Red, plus T800 and T1100) are discontinued but common on the used market. The original **Yellow** sat in the mid-launch slot and the original **Red** was the higher-launch option.

## Flex codes and swing speed

| Code | Rough equivalent | Black models | Red models |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.5 | Regular | ~90–100 mph | ~85–95 mph |
| 6.0 | Stiff | ~100–110 mph | ~95–105 mph |
| 6.5 | X-Stiff | ~110–115 mph | ~105–115 mph |
| TX | Tour X | 115+ mph | — |

Don't know your speed? A [personal launch monitor or swing speed radar](/shop/best-golf-swing-speed-tracker) costs less than a premium shaft and takes the guesswork out.

## How to choose

1. **Start with ball flight.** Too high and spinny: Black or Smoke Black. About right: Smoke Blue or Silver. Too low: Smoke Red or Red CB.
2. **Then weight.** Heavier (70 g+) helps fast, quick-tempo swingers control the face. Lighter (50–60 g) helps moderate swings gain speed.
3. **Then flex,** from the table above. If you're between flexes, smooth tempos can go softer and aggressive transitions firmer.
4. **Check the adapter.** Aftermarket shafts need the right tip sleeve for your driver head.
5. **Watch for "made for" versions.** Stock HZRDUS shafts in new drivers aren't always identical to the aftermarket models.

## Is HZRDUS worth it?

For fast swingers who fight a high, spinny ball flight or a two-way miss, a fitted HZRDUS can tighten dispersion noticeably. For moderate speeds it's often the wrong tool. A smoother, lighter shaft will usually add more distance than a "tour" label. A one-hour fitting with a launch monitor is the best money you can spend before buying any aftermarket shaft.
