**The short list:** clubs (14 maximum), 6–12 balls, tees, a ball marker, a divot tool, a glove, a towel, water, sunscreen and a hat. Add a rain layer and an umbrella if the forecast is uncertain. Everything else is a nice extra.

Keep the essentials in the same pockets every time, and you'll never be the person asking to borrow a tee on the 1st.

## Essentials (every round)

| Item | Why | How many |
|---|---|---|
| Clubs | Max **14** under the Rules (Rule 4.1b) | 7–14 |
| Golf balls | Spares for water and trees | 6–12 (beginners: a dozen+) |
| Tees | Short ones for irons and hybrids, tall ones for the driver | 10+ of each |
| Ball marker | To mark your ball on the green | 1–2 |
| Divot / pitch-mark repair tool | Fix your marks on the green | 1 |
| Glove | Grip and blister protection | 1 + a spare |
| Towel | Clean the ball and grips | 1, clipped to the bag |
| Water bottle | Rounds take 4+ hours | 1 large |
| Phone, wallet, keys | Keep in one zipped pocket | — |

Not sure which glove goes on which hand? See [which hand you wear a golf glove on](/shop/which-hand-do-you-wear-a-golf-glove).

## Weather kit

- **Sun:** sunscreen (SPF 30+), lip balm, a hat or visor, sunglasses
- **Rain:** waterproof jacket and trousers, an umbrella (62 in or larger, windproof), a dry towel in a plastic bag, spare glove
- **Cold:** a mid-layer, beanie, hand warmers, winter mitts for between shots
- **Bugs:** insect repellent, especially in the evening near water

## Useful extras

- Rangefinder or GPS watch
- Snacks: bananas, nuts, bars (the turn can be busy)
- Small first-aid kit: plasters/band-aids, blister patches, pain relief
- Pencil and scorecard holder, or a scoring app
- Alignment stick for warm-up
- Spare shoelaces and a ball retriever if your course has a lot of water

## For beginners: you need less than you think

A half set is enough to start: driver or 3-wood, a hybrid, 7-iron, 9-iron, a wedge and a putter. Buy cheap or used balls. You'll lose a few, and that's part of learning. Our [what is a golf club called](/shop/what-is-a-golf-stick-called) guide explains what each club is for.

## Before you leave home

1. Count your clubs: 14 or fewer.
2. Check the course dress code (collars, no denim, soft spikes).
3. Charge your rangefinder or GPS watch.
4. Check the tee time and arrive 20–30 minutes early.
5. Clear out old scorecards, wrappers and broken tees. A lighter bag is easier to carry.

If your bag is a mess of pockets, our guide to [setting up a golf bag](/blog/how-to-set-up-golf-bag) shows a layout that works.
