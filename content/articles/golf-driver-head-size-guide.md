The Rules of Golf cap a driver head at **460 cc**, with a small manufacturing tolerance of 10 cc. Almost every modern driver is built right up to that limit, because a bigger head means a bigger, more forgiving face. Unless you're a strong player who wants a lower-spinning, more workable driver, **460 cc is the right size for you.**

## The rules in one table

| Spec | Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Head volume | 460 cc (+10 cc tolerance) | Bigger heads are non-conforming |
| Club length (Rules of Golf) | 48 in | The standard limit |
| Club length (Model Local Rule G-10) | 46 in | Available to committees since 2022 and used on most pro tours |
| Typical stock driver length | ~45–45.75 in | Measured the USGA way |

Non-conforming drivers can't be used in competitions or for rounds you post for your handicap. That includes "oversized" heads sold for long drive or novelty use.

## Why bigger is usually better

A larger head lets designers stretch the face and push weight to the perimeter. That raises the **moment of inertia (MOI)**, the head's resistance to twisting. When you miss the centre, a high-MOI head loses less ball speed and keeps the ball closer to your target line. For a typical amateur whose strike pattern covers a good part of the face, that's free distance.

## When a smaller head makes sense

Some manufacturers make **440–450 cc** heads, usually in their low-spin or "tour" models. They can help:

- **Fast swingers** who generate too much spin and want a more penetrating flight
- **Players who shape the ball** and like a more compact, workable head
- Golfers who simply prefer a **compact look** at address

The trade-off is less forgiveness. If you don't consistently hit the middle, a smaller head will usually cost you distance on average.

## Head size isn't everything

Ball flight depends as much on **loft, shaft and length** as on volume:

- **Loft:** most amateurs launch better with 10.5° or more than with 9°. Our [driver loft guide](/shop/best-loft-driver-golf) explains why.
- **Shaft:** weight, flex and profile change launch and spin. If you swing fast, see our [HZRDUS shaft guide](/shop/best-hzrdus-golf-shafts).
- **Length:** shorter drivers are easier to hit in the centre. Learn [how to measure driver length](/blog/how-to-measure-golf-driver-length) properly.

Put some impact tape or foot spray on the face during a range session. Your strike pattern will tell you more about which driver to choose than any spec sheet.
