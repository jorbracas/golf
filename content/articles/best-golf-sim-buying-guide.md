The order you buy things in matters more than any single product. **Measure the room → choose the launch monitor → screen and enclosure → hitting mat → projector → software.** Get the first two right and the rest is easy to upgrade later. Get them wrong and you'll be returning things.

## Step 1: measure the room

You need enough **ceiling height to swing a driver**, enough **width** for your hitting position and enough **depth** for your launch monitor. Typical targets are 9–10 ft+ of ceiling, 10–12 ft of width (14 ft+ for mixed left- and right-handers) and 15–20 ft of depth. Use our [simulator room size calculator](/shop/golf-simulator-room-size). It takes two minutes and can save you a costly mistake.

## Step 2: the launch monitor (spend here first)

The launch monitor is the brain of the simulator. It decides your data quality, your room depth (camera units beside the ball need less than radar behind you) and your software options. Our [launch monitor comparison](/shop/best-launch-monitors-for-golf) covers every budget.

## Step 3: what you hit into

| Option | Cost | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net only | ~$100–$200 | Cheap, quick, portable | No projected picture |
| Impact screen in an enclosure | ~$800–$1,500+ | Full-size image, safer side netting | Needs mounting, space behind screen |
| DIY frame + premium screen | Varies | Custom fit to your room | Your time and tools |

The screen material makes a real difference to picture quality, noise and bounce-back. See our [impact screen guide](/shop/best-golf-impact-screen).

## Step 4: the hitting mat

Don't economise here. A hard, cheap mat lets the club bounce, flatters thin shots and is hard on wrists and elbows. Budget at least **$150–$300**, and more for a mat that lets you hit down on the ball naturally. Our [hitting mat guide](/shop/best-golf-hitting-mat) compares the main types.

## Step 5: the projector

For a full-size picture you want a **short-throw projector** mounted above and slightly behind the hitting area, so you don't cast a shadow. **3,000 lumens** is a starting point and **4,000+** suits screens 10 ft wide or more. Choose the aspect ratio (4:3 or 16:10/16:9) to match your screen. More in our [screen and projector guide](/shop/best-golf-screen-and-projector).

## Step 6: software

Many launch monitors come with basic practice software. Full course play usually means a subscription to the maker's own platform or third-party software, often **$100–$600 a year**. Check your launch monitor's compatibility list before you buy anything else.

## Typical budgets

| Setup | Typical total |
|---|---|
| Launch monitor + net + mat | ~$700–$1,500 |
| Starter simulator with screen and budget projector | ~$2,000–$4,000 |
| Mid-range | ~$5,000–$10,000 |
| Premium | ~$10,000–$20,000+ |

The full breakdown is in our [golf simulator cost guide](/shop/golf-simulator-cost).

## Mistakes we see most

1. **Buying before measuring.** Especially the ceiling, and especially in basements with beams.
2. **A cheap mat under an expensive monitor.** It costs you data quality and joint health.
3. **The screen too close to the wall.** It needs space to absorb impact, or balls bounce back hard.
4. **The projector in the wrong place.** Mounted in front of the golfer, it throws your shadow across the screen.
5. **Ignoring software costs** when comparing launch monitors.
