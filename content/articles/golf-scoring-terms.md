**Par** is the number of strokes a skilled golfer should need on a hole, usually 3, 4 or 5. Everything else is counted from it: **one under par is a birdie, two under an eagle, three under an albatross**. **One over is a bogey, two over a double bogey**. Below is the full list, plus the stats and slang you'll hear in any clubhouse.

## Scores relative to par

| Term | Score on the hole | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Condor | 4 under par | A hole-in-one on a par 5 (extremely rare) |
| Albatross (double eagle) | 3 under par | A 2 on a par 5 |
| Eagle | 2 under par | A 3 on a par 5, or a 2 on a par 4 |
| Birdie | 1 under par | A 3 on a par 4 |
| Par | Level | A 4 on a par 4 |
| Bogey | 1 over par | A 5 on a par 4 |
| Double bogey | 2 over par | A 6 on a par 4 |
| Triple bogey | 3 over par | A 7 on a par 4 |

A **hole-in-one** (or **ace**) is a score of 1 on any hole, usually a par 3.

## How par is set

Par depends mainly on length. As a rough guide for men, par 3s are up to about 260 yards, par 4s about 240–490 and par 5s about 450–710. Women's par uses shorter bands. Most 18-hole courses add up to a **par of 70, 71 or 72**.

Your total for the round is described relative to course par: shooting 78 on a par-72 course is "6 over". "Level" or "even" means you matched par exactly. See [what counts as a good score](/shop/what-is-a-good-golf-score).

## Gross, net and handicap

- **Gross score**: the actual number of strokes you took.
- **Net score**: gross minus your handicap strokes (your Course Handicap for that course). A 95 gross with a Course Handicap of 18 is a 77 net.
- **Handicap**: a measure of your ability that evens out competition. Estimate yours with our [handicap calculator](/shop/golf-handicap-calculator).

## Stats and terms from the round

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **GIR (green in regulation)** | Reaching the green in par minus two strokes: 1 shot on a par 3, 2 on a par 4, 3 on a par 5 |
| **Fairway hit** | Tee shot finishes in the fairway on a par 4 or 5 |
| **Up and down** | Getting the ball in the hole in two shots from off the green (chip or pitch, then one putt) |
| **Sandy** | An up and down from a greenside bunker |
| **Scrambling** | Making par or better after missing the green in regulation |
| **Pin high** | Ball finishes level with the flag in distance, even if wide |
| **Three-putt** | Taking three putts on a green, usually costly |
| **Gimme** | A short putt the other player concedes in match play or a casual game |

## Formats that change how you count

- **Stroke play**: every stroke counts and the lowest total wins.
- **Match play**: you win, lose or halve each hole; the score is shown as holes "up".
- **Stableford**: points per hole relative to par (bogey 1, par 2, birdie 3, eagle 4).
- **Scramble and best ball**: team formats. See our [foursome games guide](/shop/best-4-man-golf-games).

## Where the bird names came from

The word **birdie** comes from late 19th-century American slang, where "bird" meant something excellent; it's traced to Atlantic City Country Club in 1899. **Eagle** and **albatross** followed as bigger birds for rarer scores. The **condor** is the unofficial name for a score of four under on a hole.
