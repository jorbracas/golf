A home golf simulator can cost **under $1,000 or well over $20,000**. Realistic tiers:

- **Practice setup** (launch monitor, net, mat): **~$700–$1,500**
- **Starter simulator** (adds screen, enclosure, budget projector): **~$2,000–$4,000**
- **Mid-range** (better launch monitor, short-throw projector, premium mat): **~$5,000–$10,000**
- **Premium** (high-end photometric or overhead launch monitor, laser projector): **~$10,000–$20,000+**

The **launch monitor** is the biggest single cost and the hardest thing to upgrade later, so that's where to spend first.

## Cost breakdown by tier

| Component | Practice | Starter | Mid-range | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Launch monitor | ~$600 | $600–$700 | ~$3,000 | $7,000–$10,000+ |
| Net or screen | Net $100–$200 | Screen + enclosure $800–$1,200 | Screen $400–$600 + enclosure $800–$1,500 | Pro-grade screen and enclosure |
| Hitting mat | $150–$300 | $150–$300 | ~$600 | Premium mat |
| Projector | Phone/tablet | $500–$700 | $1,200–$2,000 short-throw | $2,000–$5,000 laser |
| **Typical total** | **$700–$1,500** | **$2,000–$4,000** | **$5,000–$10,000** | **$10,000–$20,000+** |

Examples at each level include radar units such as the Garmin Approach R10 or Rapsodo MLM2Pro at the entry level, photometric units like the Bushnell Launch Pro or SkyTrak+ mid-range, and Foresight or Uneekor systems at the top. Prices change often. Our [launch monitor guide](/shop/best-launch-monitors-for-golf) compares the options.

## Costs people forget

- **Software:** many launch monitors need a subscription for full course play and features, typically **$100–$200 a year**.
- **Computer or tablet:** some simulator software needs a capable PC for good graphics.
- **Room preparation:** flooring, electrics, lighting and sometimes moving a garage door opener, **~$500–$2,000**.
- **Replacement parts:** mats wear where you hit, and screens eventually need replacing.
- **Installation:** optional, but common for enclosures and ceiling-mounted projectors.

## Where to spend and where to save

1. **Launch monitor first.** Accurate ball and club data is the point of the whole setup. A cheap screen with a good monitor beats the reverse.
2. **The mat matters for your joints.** A poor mat bounces the club and hurts wrists and elbows. Spend more here than you'd expect.
3. **Save on the enclosure.** DIY frames and netting work well if you're handy.
4. **Projector last.** Start with a TV or tablet and add a projector once you know you'll use the sim.
