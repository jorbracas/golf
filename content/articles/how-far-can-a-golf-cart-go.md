A healthy **48 V lead-acid golf cart usually goes 15–25 miles** on a full charge. A **48 V lithium cart typically manages 30–50 miles or more**, depending on pack size. An 18-hole round is only 4–6 miles of driving, so any cart in decent shape should finish two rounds. If yours can't finish one, the batteries are the problem, not the cart.

Use the calculator below to estimate your own cart, then read on for what changes the number.

## Why lithium goes so much further

It isn't magic. It comes down to how much of the battery you can safely use:

- **Lead-acid** (flooded or AGM) should only be run down to about **50%** of its rated capacity. Going deeper shortens its life sharply, and voltage sags under load.
- **Lithium (LiFePO4)** gives you about **90%** of its rating, holds voltage until it's nearly empty and weighs a fraction as much.

So a 48 V 100 Ah lithium pack (4.8 kWh) gives you more usable energy than a much "bigger" lead-acid pack. That's why it often lands in the **35–45 mile** range on flat ground.

| 48 V pack | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Lead-acid (6 × 8 V or 4 × 12 V), healthy | 15–25 miles |
| Lithium 60 Ah | 20–30 miles |
| Lithium 100–105 Ah | 35–45 miles |
| Lithium 150–160 Ah | 45–60+ miles |

## What eats your range

- **Hills:** 20–40% less range on a hilly course or neighbourhood.
- **Load:** a full 4-seater, a cargo bed or a lift kit with oversize tyres costs 10–25%.
- **Speed:** cruising at 20 mph+ costs noticeably more than 12–15 mph.
- **Tyre pressure:** soft tyres are an easy few percent lost. Check them monthly.
- **Cold:** both chemistries lose capacity in cold weather. Lithium packs often limit charging below freezing.
- **Accessories:** light bars, sound systems and heaters all draw from the same pack.
- **Age:** a 5–7-year-old lead-acid pack may only have a fraction of its original range left. Our guide on [how to tell if a golf cart battery is bad](/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-golf-cart-battery-is-bad) walks you through the tests.

## Getting more range from the cart you have

1. **Charge after every use** (lead-acid). Don't store it half-empty.
2. **Check water levels** in flooded batteries monthly and top up with distilled water after charging.
3. **Clean and tighten the terminals.** Corrosion is resistance, and resistance is wasted energy.
4. **Keep tyres at the recommended pressure**, usually printed on the sidewall.
5. **Drive smoothly.** Hard launches and constant full throttle drain any battery.
6. **If the pack is old anyway, look at lithium.** A [lithium conversion](/blog/convert-golf-cart-to-lithium-battery) usually costs less than people expect once you count a lead-acid replacement plus water, maintenance and weight. You'll also need a [lithium-compatible charger](/shop/best-golf-cart-lithium-battery-charger).

## How we estimate range

The calculator multiplies pack voltage by amp-hours to get the energy (kWh). It then applies a usable share (90% lithium, 50% lead-acid) and divides by an assumed consumption of about **120 Wh per mile** for a two-seater on flat ground at normal speeds. Terrain, passengers, lift kits and speed raise that figure. Treat the result as a planning estimate, not a guarantee.
