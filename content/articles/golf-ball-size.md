A golf ball must be **at least 1.680 inches (42.67 mm) in diameter** and weigh **no more than 1.620 ounces (45.93 g)**. Those two limits come from the Rules of Equipment written by The R&A and the USGA. Almost every ball on sale sits right on both: as small and as heavy as the rules allow, because that flies furthest.

## Golf ball dimensions at a glance

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Minimum diameter | 1.680 in (42.67 mm) |
| Maximum weight | 1.620 oz (45.93 g) |
| Circumference (at 1.68 in) | ~5.28 in (13.4 cm) |
| Volume (at 1.68 in) | ~2.48 cu in (40.7 cm³) |
| Dimples | usually about 300–400, depending on the model |

There is **no maximum diameter and no minimum weight**. A larger or lighter ball is legal, just slower and shorter, so nobody sells one for normal play.

## The other rules a ball must meet

Size and weight aren't the only tests. A conforming ball must also:

- **Not exceed the initial velocity limit** when struck by the governing bodies' test machine.
- **Not exceed the overall distance standard**, a cap on carry plus roll in a standard test.
- **Behave like a symmetrical sphere**. Balls designed to correct a slice by flying differently depending on orientation are not allowed.

Balls that pass appear on the **List of Conforming Golf Balls**. Many competitions require you to use a listed ball, through a Local Rule adopted by the committee.

## The "small ball" history

Until 1990, two sizes were in use. The British "small ball" measured **1.62 inches** and was popular in windy links golf because it bored through the air. The American ball was 1.68 inches. The R&A adopted the 1.68 in minimum in 1990, so the small ball is no longer legal.

## How the ball compares to the hole

The hole is **4¼ inches (108 mm)** in diameter, about **2.5 times** the width of the ball. That's why a putt that dies right on the edge can still drop: it falls once the ball's centre of gravity gets past the lip.

## Why dimples matter

A smooth ball would fly roughly half as far. Dimples create a thin layer of turbulent air that clings to the ball, which reduces drag. Combined with backspin, they also create lift. The number of dimples matters less than their shape and pattern, which is why counts vary between about 300 and 400 on modern balls.

## Size isn't what makes balls different

Since all conforming balls are the same size, the differences come from **construction**: number of layers, core compression and cover material. Urethane covers spin more around the greens; ionomer covers are tougher and spin less. That's what separates a [low-spin ball](/shop/best-low-spin-golf-balls) from a tour ball.
