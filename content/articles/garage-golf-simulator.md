A garage is the most common place for a home simulator because it has the height and depth most rooms don't. You need roughly a **9–10 ft ceiling** where you swing, **10–14 ft of width** and about **15–18 ft of depth**. Add a **retractable screen** and the car can still park there.

## The space you need

| Dimension | Minimum | Comfortable |
|---|---|---|
| Ceiling height | ~8.5–9 ft (shorter golfers, compact swings) | 10 ft+ (golfers over 6 ft: 10 ft or more) |
| Width | ~10 ft (right- or left-handed only) | 14 ft+ if left- and right-handers share it |
| Depth | ~15 ft (tight) | 18 ft+ (about 10–12 ft from ball to screen, plus room behind) |

Measure the ceiling **at your swing position, not the highest point**. Lights, beams, storage racks and the garage door often sit lower. Our [room size calculator](/shop/golf-simulator-room-size) checks your numbers.

## One-car vs two-car garages

- **One-car (typically ~12 × 20 ft)**: workable but tight. Fine for a right-handed golfer with a compact setup; cramped if you want side room or a sofa.
- **Two-car**: the ideal. Dedicate one bay to the simulator and park in the other, or centre the hitting area for more side room.

## The garage door problem

The door is the most common deal-breaker:

- **When open**, the door panels and tracks hang below the ceiling, often right where you swing.
- **The opener** usually sits in the middle of the ceiling.

The common fixes are to set up with the door closed and confirm clearance, place the hitting area forward of the door tracks, or switch to a **wall-mounted (jackshaft) opener**, which removes the opener from the middle of the ceiling. The door tracks and the open door still need clearance.

## Retractable screens: keep the car

A retractable impact screen rolls up into a housing, like a projector screen. Combined with a hitting mat you can slide aside and a ceiling-mounted projector, it lets the garage work as both a simulator and a garage.

- **Pros**: car still fits, cleaner look, protects the screen from dust when not in use.
- **Cons**: costs more than a fixed screen, and must be fully lowered and tensioned before every session.
- **Tip**: add side netting or a ceiling net so mishits don't hit the car or the door.

Compare options in our [impact screen guide](/shop/best-golf-impact-screen).

## Flooring, cold and damp

- **Floor**: concrete is hard on joints. Use a quality hitting mat on a foam underlay or rubber tiles, over a vapour barrier if the slab gets damp.
- **Cold**: you'll want a heater in winter. A cold ball and a stiff body produce less ball speed, and the launch monitor measures that, so you will see shorter shots. What the simulator ignores is the denser cold air.
- **Damp**: condensation is bad for electronics. Store the launch monitor and computer inside, and consider a dehumidifier.
- **Dust**: projectors suck in dust; clean filters regularly.

## Which launch monitor works in a garage?

- **Camera-based units** sit beside or above the ball and need less depth.
- **Radar units** sit behind you and need extra room behind the ball. Check the manufacturer's minimum distances.

## What it costs

As rough estimates: a practice setup with a budget launch monitor, net and mat can come in **under $1,500**. A starter simulator with screen, enclosure and projector is typically **$2,000–4,000**, and mid-range builds **$5,000–10,000**. A retractable screen, garage-door opener swap or flooring adds to that.
